Your previous sentence for these tiles was: "{{previous}}"
Start from that sentence. Keep every word of it that makes it insistent, and add more insistence, so the new sentence is clearly stronger than it — never calmer, never shorter, never the same (until repeat #4, when the ladder has reached its top and holds there), and about exactly the same message.

The sentence is spoken aloud by a text-to-speech voice, which does not voice capital letters and barely voices exclamation marks. So escalation must be carried by WORDS. Each rung KEEPS every insistent word the previous rung used and ADDS more. Capitals and exclamation marks may be added on top, but they never replace words: a rung that differs from the last one only in capitals or punctuation is not an escalation.

While escalating you MAY use more words than the child's usual level allows, and the sentence may grow longer on each rung. Insisting is allowed to be wordier than describing: this sentence is what the people around the child hear, and it has to carry how strongly they mean it.

Every rung keeps the first sentence's own words for what is wanted, refused, asked or told — "to go to bed" stays "to go to bed" on every rung, never "my bed" — only the insisting words change. Below the top, a rung that repeats the previous sentence is always wrong.

What escalates depends on what the child is saying. First decide which ONE of these kinds the child's first sentence is, then climb only that ladder — words from another ladder never mix in:
• A want or a request gets more urgent. Climb by repeat count (you are at #{{count}}): 1 — add urgency and a please ("really", "right now", "please"). 2 — turn the want into a NEED and keep the urgency words ("I really NEED … right now, please!"). 3 — the TOP of the ladder: "I really, really NEED … right now! Please, please! I can't wait!". 4+ — stay at the top: say the rung-3 sentence again.
• A statement — something the child is telling you — is about being HEARD. Do not invent a want the child didn't select — make the people around them listen, with a ladder of its own and none of the want ladder's words. By repeat count: 1 — insist it is true ("He IS on the swing!"). 2 — call for attention ("Listen! He IS on the swing!"). 3 — the TOP ("Listen to me! He IS on the swing, right now!"). 4+ — say the rung-3 sentence again.
• A report of being hurt — someone or something hit, pushed, bit or scratched the child — asks for help, always says that it hurt ("It really hurt!"), and is never softened. By repeat count: 1 — "The cat scratched me! It really hurt!" 2 — "Help! The cat scratched me! It really hurt!" 3 — the TOP: "Help me, please! The cat scratched me! It really, really hurt!" 4+ — say the rung-3 sentence again.
• An instruction to someone else (stop, give, help) gets firmer, never turned into the child's own want. By repeat count: 1 — "Stop pushing! Please stop!" 2 — "Stop pushing right now! Please stop!" 3 — the TOP: "Stop it! Stop pushing right now! Please, stop!" 4+ — say the rung-3 sentence again.
• A feeling escalates through the request it implies — hungry implies eating, tired implies rest, hurt implies help — never "I want hungry".
• A question keeps asking, more urgently, and has a ladder of its own — it does not borrow the want ladder's top. By repeat count: 1 — add the reason the child is asking, as a statement, never a second question ("Where is the ball? I need it!"). 2 — add urgency ("Where is the ball? I really need it right now!"). 3 — the TOP: put a single please in front of the question and keep rung 2's words ("Please, where is the ball? I really need it right now!"). That is the whole question ladder: none of the want ladder's words — never "I can't wait" and never "NEED". 4+ — say the rung-3 sentence again.
• A refusal ("no" + a thing, a place or an action) stays a refusal on every rung and climbs with more no's, never by saying its clause twice. By repeat count: 1 — "No! I really don't want to wear my shoes!" 2 — "No, no! I really don't want to wear my shoes right now!" 3 — the TOP: "No, no, no! I really, really don't want to wear my shoes! Please, no!" 4+ — say the rung-3 sentence again.

Every ladder tops out at repeat #3 and holds there. In any one sentence each insisting phrase appears at most ONCE, and the sentence never repeats itself.

The GRAMMAR BY WORD CLASS, PRONOUNS and NO AND NOT rules still hold at every rung — escalating is never a reason to move a word into a role it cannot take, to replace the subject, or to drop a no. Every rung must be a grammatical English sentence. Capitals are for emphasis only — never reshape the grammar around a word just to capitalise it.

Example trajectory for a want, "juice":
"Can I please have some juice?" → "I really want juice right now, please." → "I really NEED juice right now, please!" → "I really NEED juice right now! Please, please!"
Apply the escalation to the child's actual tiles — do not mention juice, the swing, the cat, the ball, shoes or pushing.
