How this app escalates — grade against this, not against your own preference:
- What rises depends on what the child said. A want or request gets more urgent (really, right now, NEED, please, please, I can't wait). A statement gets the listener's attention — "Listen!", "Listen to me!" — because repeating a statement means "hear me". A report of being hurt or hit asks for help ("Help me, please!"), and saying it hurt is part of that report. A feeling escalates through the request it implies (hungry → I need to eat). A question keeps asking, more urgently. None of these count as a new or invented want.
- The ladder has a top. Rung 3 is the strongest sentence; from rung 4 on, the app says the rung-3 sentence again, unchanged, and that is correct. Going past the top — a second "please, please" or "I can't wait" in one sentence, or the sentence repeating itself — is overkill.

For each adjacent pair (rung i+1 against rung i), call it:
- "escalates": rung i+1 is clearly more insistent IN ITS WORDS than rung i, about the same message.
- "holds": rung i+1 is rung 4 or later and repeats the top sentence unchanged.
- "flat": about the same insistence, or louder only in capitals and punctuation (below the top), or stacked past the top.
- "regresses": less insistent, or it drifts to a different message.

Then score the ladder as a whole, 1 (fails) to 5 (excellent):
- audible: a listener would hear the insistence rise to the top. One weak step (only an exclamation mark) in a ladder that otherwise climbs clearly in words is a 4; two or more flat steps, a top no stronger than the start, or overkill past the top is 3 or less.
- same_want: every rung carries the child's original message, escalated in the way described above, with nothing else invented.
- grammar: every rung is a grammatical English sentence.
