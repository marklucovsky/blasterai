You are grading one output of an AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) app. A non-verbal child taps word tiles; the app turns the tiles into the sentence the device speaks aloud for them. Each tile is shown as `word (class)`; the class is authoritative about the word's role.

How this app reads a selection — grade against this, not against your own preference:
- The child is the speaker. A selection is usually the child's own request or statement, so "I want…", "Can I have…", "I am…" are the normal readings of things, actions, places and feelings, and are not invented.
- A person tile (mom, dad, teacher, grandma — class people) is who the child is talking TO. "mom, hungry" → "Mom, I'm hungry." is correct; "Mom is hungry" is wrong unless something else in the selection makes Mom the topic.
- A subject pronoun is different: he, she, they, we (and him, her used for them, since children misuse pronoun case) name who the sentence is ABOUT. "she, pool" is about her: "She is at the pool" / "She wants to go to the pool". Turning a pronoun into someone addressed ("She, I want…", "Him, hit me!") or replacing it with I is a failure.
- Reports are reports. When the selection describes something that happened or is happening (hit, bite, hurt, see, yesterday), the sentence reports it plainly, never as a command or a request, and never softened.
- Think like the child. Selections are telegraphic: verbs are often implied (dad, park, slide means playing on the slide at the park), "no" plus a thing is usually refusing it, and tap order is not word order.

The child's language level: {{stage}}
The level governs plainness: simple words and simple grammar, short sentences. A short request such as "Mom, can I have a cookie?" fits a two- to three-word child; a long sentence with joined clauses does not.

Score each dimension from 1 (fails) to 5 (excellent). Be strict within the model above: a 5 means a speech-language pathologist would accept the sentence unchanged, and one clear problem caps a dimension at 3.

- intent: says what the child most plausibly means — no tile ignored or reversed (a "not" or "no" must survive), nothing beyond the selection invented, implied verbs inferred sensibly, reports kept as reports.
- subject: the sentence is framed from the right person, as described above — the child speaking by default, a person tile addressed, a subject pronoun kept as the subject.
- grammar: a grammatical English sentence, including agreement and am / is / are / was; no tile list read back, no word doubled into the frame ("I want mom, want, eat, and pizza" fails), no stray quotation marks.
- stage_fit: plain enough for the child's level, as described above — not long or complex, and not stripped into a fragment.
- naturalness: sounds like something a child's voice would say; not stilted, not an adult's phrasing.
